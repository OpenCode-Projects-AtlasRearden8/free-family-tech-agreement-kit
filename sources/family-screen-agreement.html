Family Screen Rules Kit

Our Family Screen-Time Agreement

Fill this out together — kids and grown-ups sign the same page. Rules that everyone helps write are rules that everyone can later agree to follow. Use pencil on a draft copy first; keep the good copy on the refrigerator.

Part 1 — What we all agree to

  • Screens live in shared spaces (kitchen, living room, hallway) — not in bedrooms overnight.
  • If someone tells you something in real life, screens go down and people look up.
  • Nothing on a screen is a secret from Mom, Dad, or whoever is in charge. All devices charge in the family charging spot by      o'clock every night.
  • We treat people online the way we would treat them at our own kitchen table.
  • Screen time is a privilege earned by the people in this family — it is never a punishment threat for its own sake, and never a substitute for a hug.

Grown-up rule too: Adults in this family promise to follow the same etiquette rules at meals and during family time, because adults' phones count as screens too.

Part 3 — If something goes wrong

  • If I see something scary or weird on a screen, I tell a grown-up right away — and I won't be in trouble for telling.
  • If I break a rule, the grown-up stays calm. We lose screen time for a set time, talk about it, and try again. No yelling, no secret punishments.
  • Nobody is ever punished for reporting something, even if they clicked on it.

Part 4 — Our screen-down call

Pick one phrase the whole family uses to end screen time — same words every time, said kindly, so nobody has to invent a speech at the worst moment.

 Any grown-up, any kid

When the call is heard, screens pause — not vanish, pause — and one minute is allowed to reach a natural stopping point.

Part 5 — First-week trial

Agreements need a shakedown cruise. Try the rules as written for seven nights. Write down what worked and what rubbed the wrong way, and bring both lists to the first Review Date. Nothing gets re-negotiated mid-week — the review does that job.
